# Framework: Voice and Style

Voice is the personality of the prose, the distinctive way one mind perceives and speaks. It comes from committing fully to a specific consciousness. In first person it's the character's; in third it's a blend of narrator and character.

Free indirect style: narration slides into the character's idiom without quotation marks or "he thought." "The cake sat there, sweating, obscene." The judgment is the character's, in the narrator's voice. Master this above almost any other technique. The character's state saturates the prose itself.

Filtering kills close voice. Cut "she saw," "he felt," "she noticed." Deliver the experience direct: "The door opened," "Fear closed his throat."

Sentence craft: vary length and rhythm, long build then short punch. Concrete nouns and active verbs over adjectives and adverbs. A weak verb propped by an adverb hides a better verb.

Show and tell, both: dramatize the important and emotional moments, summarize the rest. Amateurs tell everything; the overcorrected show everything.

Specificity: the one telling detail beats ten generic ones or a paragraph of abstraction. Select, don't pile.

Restraint: kill your darlings. Clear window most of the time, stained glass only where earned. Avoid overwriting, filtering, and throat-clearing transitions.
