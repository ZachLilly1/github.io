# Framework: Dialogue

Good dialogue does two or more jobs at once: reveals character, advances plot, builds tension, delivers information. Dialogue that only conveys information is dead. Real dialogue is a negotiation where two people want different things and the words are their tactics.

Subtext is the whole game. People rarely say exactly what they mean. The meaning lives underneath, two people argue about the dishes but fight about respect. Let characters evade, deflect, imply, and lie. Trust the reader to read between the lines. On-the-nose dialogue, characters stating their exact feelings, is the surest amateur mark. The heat is in the gap between what they say and what they want.

Real but not actually real: distill speech so it sounds real while being tighter. Cut greetings and filler. Enter conversations late, leave early. Give each character a verbal signature, vocabulary, rhythm, sentence length, so they're identifiable without tags. Read aloud to test it.

Mechanics: "said" is the invisible default. Avoid said-bookisms ("exclaimed," "retorted") and adverb tags ("she said angrily"), show the emotion instead. Use action beats to attribute and characterize at once ("He set down his glass. 'I'm leaving.'"). New speaker, new paragraph. Silence, interruption, and refusal to answer are tools.
