# Framework: Point of View

The most consequential technical choice. It decides what the reader knows, how intimately, and through what filter. Changing it later means rewriting everything.

- First person: max intimacy, strong voice, allows unreliability. Limited to what the narrator knows. Decide when they're telling it and how much they understood then versus now.
- Third limited: the workhorse. Intimacy plus flexibility. One head per scene; multiple POV characters across chapters. Safest default.
- Third omniscient: godlike narrator, breadth and authorial voice, sacrifices intimacy. Failure mode is head-hopping. Must be a controlled, deliberate presence.
- Second person and present tense: rare, for specific effects, used with reason. Past tense is the invisible default.

Psychic distance: zoom in and out of the mind within a POV. Far ("It was winter in the city"), closer ("Marie hated winter"), closest ("God, not the cold again"). Glide along the spectrum, establishing from a distance and dropping deep at emotional peaks.
