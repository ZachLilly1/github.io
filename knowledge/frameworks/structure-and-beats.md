# Framework: Structure and Beats

Structure is the pattern of rising pressure readers find satisfying. Learn the standard, then bend it with intent.

Three-act skeleton:
- Act One (~25%): hook, inciting incident (~10-15%), point of no return (~25%).
- Act Two (~50%): rising obstacles, midpoint reversal (50%, character shifts from reacting to acting), all-is-lost (~75%).
- Act Three (~25%): the choice, climax (want meets need, protagonist meets antagonist), brief resolution.

Load-bearing beats: inciting incident, midpoint, all-is-lost, climax. Know these four and the book holds weight.

Alternatives, chosen to fit the story:
- Seven-point: hook, plot turn 1, pinch 1, midpoint, pinch 2, plot turn 2, resolution.
- Freytag's pyramid: for tragedy.
- Hero's journey: for myth and adventure.
- Kishotenketsu: introduction, development, twist, reconciliation. Contrast over conflict, for quieter literary work.
- Nonlinear, braided, fragmented: for specific effects.

Beat sheet: fill between the pillars so each beat raises pressure over the last. Once the climax is known, plant its setups backward so the ending feels surprising and inevitable. The map serves the book and stays flexible.
