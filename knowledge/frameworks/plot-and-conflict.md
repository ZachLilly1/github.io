# Framework: Plot and Conflict

Story is chronological events. Plot is their arrangement for effect. Power often lives in the gap between them: what you reveal, when, and in what order.

Conflict runs on three levels at once: internal (self), interpersonal (character vs character), external (society, nature, fate). Every scene needs at least one active conflict. Slack scene means nobody wants anything or the wanting isn't opposed.

Escalation via the try-fail cycle: attempt, fail in a way that worsens the situation, try again against steeper odds. Try, fail, try harder, fail worse. Each setback costs more and narrows options. Distinguish new events from new pressure. Motion isn't escalation. Ask of every scene whether the pressure is higher than the last.

Cause and effect: scenes connect by "therefore" and "but," never "and then." Each scene is the consequence of the last and the cause of the next.

Subplots: echo, contrast, or complicate the main plot and explore the theme from another angle. Each has its own arc and intersects the spine.

Setups and payoffs: plant early, pay off late. Chekhov's gun. Engineer the feeling of inevitability by planting backward from the climax.

Pacing: rhythm of tension and release. Speed up by cutting in late, leaving early, shortening sentences, stacking events. Slow down by expanding a moment, adding interiority. Vary the tempo.
