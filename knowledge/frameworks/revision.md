# Framework: Revision

Novels are rewritten, not written. The draft is raw material; the book is made in revision. Run passes largest to smallest. Never line-edit a scene you might cut.

Structural pass: does the whole thing work? Read the full draft through, taking notes without editing, to see macro problems. Is the arc complete and earned? Does the plot escalate or plateau? Are stakes clear and rising? Does every scene justify itself? Any plot holes, saggy stretches, unmotivated actions, unearned ending? May mean cutting characters, adding scenes, restructuring, rewriting the ending. Before any prose work.

Scene pass: does each scene have a POV goal, conflict, and turn? Enter late, leave early? Advance plot and reveal character? In the right place? Cut scenes that only repeat or spin without turning. Combine scenes doing the same work. Connect by therefore and but, never and then.

Line edit: sharpen sentences, vary rhythm, cut filler and filtering, strengthen verbs, remove clichés, fix awkward constructions. Tighten dialogue, cut the on-the-nose. Hunt your own repeated tics. Read aloud.

Copyedit: last, when content is locked. Grammar, punctuation, spelling, consistency (names, timeline, invented terms). Fresh eyes matter, distance or another reader.

Feedback: beta readers tell you where they got bored or confused, trust their diagnosis over their prescription. When multiple readers flag the same spot, believe them even if they disagree on the fix. Receive criticism without defensiveness; hold your vision where feedback conflicts with intent.
