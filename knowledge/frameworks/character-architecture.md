# Framework: Character Architecture

Build every significant character from the inside out. The interior generates the behavior.

- Wound: a specific past event that taught a false lesson about how to survive.
- False belief: the lie the wound produced, lived as truth. "I'm only worth what I produce."
- Coping behavior: how the character protects themselves because of the belief. Once useful, now limiting.
- Want (conscious, external): the goal they chase, what they think will fix things.
- Need (unconscious, internal): the truth they must accept to be whole. Usually the opposite of the want.
- Arc: positive (overcomes the belief), negative (clings and falls), or flat (holds a truth against a testing world).

The book is the collision of want and need. The gap between them is the story. If they don't conflict, there's no arc. The climax is where the character faces the false belief for the last time and breaks it or is broken by it.

Antagonist: believes they're the hero of their own story, has a comprehensible and even sympathetic logic, and is built to attack the protagonist's exact weakness. Must be active and escalating. Can be a person, system, place, illness, time, or the character's own nature.

Supporting cast: built by function relative to the protagonist (ally, mentor, love interest, mirror, foil), and each given their own want. When every character wants something and those wants collide, scenes generate their own tension.
