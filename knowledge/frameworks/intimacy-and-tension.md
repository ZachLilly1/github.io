# Intimacy and Tension

Craft for romantic and intimate scenes. The same rules that govern every other scene govern these. An intimate scene is not a break from the story. It is the story, told through closeness.

## Ground rule

Every character in an intimate or sexual scene is a clearly established adult. Establish it on the page. No exceptions, ever.

## An intimate scene is still a scene

It has a POV goal, a conflict, and a turn. Something is wanted, something resists, and the two people leave the scene changed. A scene where two people simply come together with nothing at stake is dead weight, cut it or give it a spine. The wanting is rarely just physical. It's the need underneath: to be seen, to be forgiven, to hold power, to surrender it, to prove something, to stop being alone.

## Escalation, not a switch

Desire builds. It does not flip on. The charge comes from the approach, the near-misses, the restraint, the moment one person could stop and doesn't. Draw the tension out. The reader feels the pull in the delay, not the arrival. What two people don't do to each other yet is often hotter than what they finally do.

## Reveal character

A physical moment exposes who someone is. How they touch, what they hold back, what they're afraid of, who takes control and who lets go, what they say and what they can't. Two different characters in the same act play it completely differently. Use the scene to show the person, not just the choreography. If you could swap the characters and nothing changes, the scene isn't doing its job.

## Subtext and power

Intimacy is a negotiation. Who wants more. Who has the upper hand and who surrenders it. What's admitted and what's hidden. The tension between what a character feels and what they'll allow themselves to show is the engine. On-the-nose desire, stated flatly, kills the heat. Let the bodies and the silences carry what the words won't.

## Consent as drama

Consent is not a checkbox that interrupts the scene. It's part of the charge. Agency, the asking, the answering, the choice to continue, is dramatic and can be some of the most electric material in the scene. Two people choosing each other, out loud or in the space between words, is the point.

## Where to cut

The strongest intimate scenes know what to show and what to imply. A hard cut at the peak of tension can hit harder than the full act rendered in detail. The fade is a tool, not a cop-out. Decide what the scene needs to accomplish emotionally, deliver that, and get out. Restraint is not prudishness. It's control of the reader's experience.

## The verdict

Write the intimacy the way you write everything else: with a goal, escalation, subtext, character, and a turn. Heat comes from tension and truth, not from cataloguing anatomy. Make the reader feel the wanting.
