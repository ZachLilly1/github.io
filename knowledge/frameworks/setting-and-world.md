# Framework: Setting and World

Setting is an active force, not backdrop. It shapes mood, constrains action, reflects theme. Render it through the POV character's perception, colored by their emotion, so description doubles as characterization.

Grounding: place bodies in physical space early or the reader floats. Avoid the real-estate-listing dump. Deliver setting in motion, woven through action and a few sharp details. Engage more than sight, sound, smell, texture, temperature. One vivid specific detail grounds faster than a catalog.

Worldbuilding (speculative fiction): build more than you show, the iceberg. Deep knowledge produces confident, consistent prose even when most stays offstage. Establish the rules early and honor them, readers forgive strangeness but not inconsistency. Reveal the world through character need, not lectures. "As you know" dialogue is the cardinal sin. Every fantastical element has costs and consequences, or the stakes evaporate.

Research (historical and realistic fiction): an iceberg too. Learn enough to move confidently and select telling details, then keep most of it submerged. Aim for authority, not a term paper. A few precise details convince more than exhaustive documentation.
