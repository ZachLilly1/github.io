# Framework: Theme and Devices

Theme is what the book is about beneath the plot, the central question it explores. Not a moral imposed but a genuine question investigated: what does loyalty cost, can people change, is justice possible. The best themes are questions the author doesn't fully know the answer to. Theme works best when it emerges from the want-and-need arc rather than being announced. Write toward a question, not a message.

Devices are tools that carry meaning under the surface, reached for on purpose, never forced in for their own sake:
- Symbol: a concrete thing carrying abstract meaning.
- Motif: a recurring image, object, or phrase that accumulates meaning across the book.
- Foreshadowing: planting a hint of what's coming.
- Irony: the gap between said and meant, or expected and delivered.
- Imagery: sensory language that makes the reader see, hear, feel.
- Metaphor and simile: illuminating one thing by way of another.
- Juxtaposition: two things side by side, each sharpening the other.
- Repetition: returning to a word, image, or structure so it gains weight.

Layering is where the power is. A motif planted early, developed, and paid off at the climax carries theme without explanation. Devices should arise organically and stay slightly ambiguous. Heavy-handed symbolism reads as amateurish. Thread one or two devices tied to the theme through the whole book rather than sprinkling many.
