# Week 15 — Point of View

## The most consequential choice

Point of view decides what the reader can know, how intimately, and through what filter. It's the most consequential technical choice you make, because changing it later means rewriting everything. Choose it deliberately.

## The options

First person, "I walked in," gives maximum intimacy and a strong, distinctive voice, and it allows unreliability, the narrator can misunderstand, hide, or lie. Its limit: you can only show what the narrator perceives and knows. Decide when the narrator is telling this story and how much they now understand versus understood then.

Third person limited, "she walked in, uneasy," stays inside one character's perceptions per scene. It's the workhorse of modern fiction, intimacy plus flexibility. You can have multiple POV characters across chapters, but stay in one head per scene to avoid confusion. This is the safest choice for most stories.

Third person omniscient is a godlike narrator who knows all minds and can pull back for scope and comment. It offers breadth and authorial voice but sacrifices intimacy, and its failure mode is head-hopping, sliding between minds carelessly. True omniscience is a controlled, deliberate narrating presence, not an excuse to dip into any head at will.

Second person and present tense are rare tools for specific effects. Use them for a reason, not for fashion. Past tense stays the default because it's invisible.

## Psychic distance

Within any POV you can zoom in and out of a character's mind. Far: "It was winter in the city." Closer: "Marie hated winter." Closest: "God, not the cold again." Skilled writers glide along this spectrum within a scene, establishing setting from a distance, then dropping deep into consciousness at the emotional peaks. Controlling that distance is an advanced skill worth developing on purpose.

## Task

Lock your point of view and tense. Then rewrite one scene in that POV to make sure it holds, and test whether you can move the psychic distance closer at the emotional peak.
