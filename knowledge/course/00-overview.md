# The Course: Overview

This is the full novel-writing curriculum the agent teaches and applies. Twenty-four lessons across six stages. The agent uses these to know how to guide a writer and how to build a book.

The arc: conceive it, populate it, structure it, write it at the sentence level, then finish it.

- Weeks 1-4: Foundation and concept. What a novel is, the premise, the genre, locking the concept.
- Weeks 5-8: Character. Protagonist architecture, desire and voice, antagonist and cast, the collision of wants.
- Weeks 9-12: Structure. Why structure holds, the scene, alternative frameworks, the full beat sheet.
- Weeks 13-16: Plot and narrative craft. Conflict and escalation, subplots and payoffs, point of view, voice on the page.
- Weeks 17-20: Prose, dialogue, devices, world. The sentence, subtext, literary devices, setting and theme and drafting.
- Weeks 21-24: Draft, revise, finish. The hard middle, the structural pass, the line edit, the finish.

Core belief: a novel is not talent poured onto a page. It is a discipline, run daily, until the thing is done. The frameworks are tools. Discipline plus tools finishes books.
