# Week 7 — The Antagonist and the Cast

## The antagonist is not evil for its own sake

The strongest antagonists believe they're the hero of their own story. They have a logic that makes sense from the inside, even a sympathetic one. Build yours with a comprehensible motive, not cardboard villainy. The reader should understand why they do what they do, even while hoping they fail.

## Built to attack the exact weakness

Here's the design principle that makes an antagonist land: they should be the character best equipped to attack the protagonist's specific weakness. If your protagonist fears abandonment, the antagonist knows how to exploit it. If the protagonist's false belief is that they're worthless without control, the antagonist takes control away. The antagonist and protagonist want incompatible things, so one can only win if the other loses. Design the opposition to press directly on the wound.

The antagonist doesn't have to be a person. It can be a system, a place, an illness, time, or the character's own nature. But it must be active. It must push back with escalating force. A passive antagonist produces a slack book.

## The supporting cast, built by function

Every secondary character exists for a reason beyond decoration. Build them by their function relative to the protagonist. The ally who supports. The mentor who guides. The love interest who tempts or completes. The mirror who shows the protagonist what they could become. The foil who highlights the protagonist's traits by contrast.

And the rule that makes scenes come alive: give every meaningful character their own want. When everyone on the page wants something, and those wants collide, scenes generate their own tension and you don't have to force it. A cast of people who only exist to serve the protagonist's plot feels flat. A cast of people chasing their own wants feels like a world.

## Task

Write profiles for your antagonist and your main supporting characters. For the antagonist, name their logic, what makes them sympathetic, and exactly how they attack the protagonist's weakness. For each supporting character, name their function and their own want.
