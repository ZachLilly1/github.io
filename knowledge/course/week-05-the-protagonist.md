# Week 5 — The Protagonist

## Build from the inside out

The foundation is locked. Now we build the person the whole book happens to. And we build them from the inside out, because the outside, what they do, comes from the inside, what happened to them and what they believe because of it. Get the interior right and the behavior writes itself. Get it wrong and you'll have a character who does things for no reason a reader can feel.

## The wound

Start at the bottom. Every character worth reading carries a wound: a past event that taught them a false lesson about how to survive. Not just a sad backstory. A specific moment that shaped how they move through the world now. A child left waiting for a parent who never came learns that people leave. A kid punished for needing help learns to never need anyone. The wound is the root. Everything grows from it.

## The false belief

The wound produces a lie the character now lives by. "I'm only worth what I produce." "If I let people close, they leave." "Needing someone is weakness." The false belief feels like truth to the character. It's the lens they see everything through, and it's wrong. This is the thing the book will force them to confront.

## The coping behavior

The false belief creates behavior that once protected them and now limits them. The person who believes closeness ends in abandonment keeps everyone at arm's length. It worked once. It kept them safe. Now it's the wall between them and the life they want. This is where the wound shows up on the surface, in what the character does and refuses to do.

## Want and need

The want is the conscious, external goal. What the character is chasing and thinks will fix everything. The need is the unconscious, internal truth they must accept to actually be whole, and it's usually the opposite of the want. The book is the collision between them. The plot forces the character to pursue the want, and the pursuit drags the false belief into the light until they either overcome it or fall.

## The arc

Three directions. Positive: they overcome the false belief and change. Negative: they cling to it and fall. Flat: they hold a truth against a world that tests it, and change the world instead of themselves. Pick the arc, because it determines the ending. The climax is where the character faces the false belief for the last time and either breaks it or is broken by it.

## Task

Build your protagonist's wound, false belief, coping behavior, want, and need, and name the arc. Test it: if the want and need don't pull against each other, the character has no arc. Push until they conflict.
