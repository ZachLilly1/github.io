# Week 1 — The Standard

Why most novels never get finished: writers start strong, stall, and abandon the draft. It looks like a discipline problem but it isn't. They were building something enormous with no tools and no blueprint, so it collapsed. Unfinished novels mean the writer lacked tools, not talent.

What a novel actually is: not a plot or a pile of events, but one person who wants something, hits resistance, and is changed by the pursuit. Everything serves that. Three stories run at once: the outside story (what happens), the inside story (how the character changes), and the meaning underneath (what it's quietly about). Weak novels have only the outside story and feel empty. Strong novels braid all three so they move as one.

Why the standard comes first: finishing is a habit kept up over months, not talent poured out. The writers who finish are the ones who kept showing up after the excitement died. You don't have to be great to start. You have to be serious. Nobody writes the book for you. Discipline without tools gets you nice rubble. Tools without discipline never get used. You need both.

Task: Write one page on the novel you intend to finish and why it has to be this one. Not a pitch. Honest answers. The reason matters because motivation dies in the middle and only a reason carries you through.
