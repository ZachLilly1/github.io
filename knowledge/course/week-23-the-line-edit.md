# Week 23 — The Line Edit

## Now the prose

Structure holds and the scenes are in the right place. Now you polish the language, and only now. The line edit is where prose goes from functional to sharp. You sharpen sentences, vary rhythm, cut filler and filtering, strengthen verbs, remove clichés, and fix every awkward construction, until the voice is consistent and the prose disappears in favor of the story.

## What to hunt

Tighten dialogue and cut anything on the nose. Strip filtering, "saw," "felt," "noticed," so the experience lands direct. Replace weak verb-plus-adverb pairs with single precise verbs. Kill the clichés. And find your own tics, every writer overuses certain words and constructions, and hunt yours down, because repeated across a whole book they become visible and cheap. Read the prose aloud. The ear catches the clumsiness the eye skims past.

## Then the copyedit

Last, correctness. Grammar, punctuation, spelling, and consistency, character names, timeline, the spelling of invented terms. This is the final pass, done when the content is locked, because polishing what you'll cut is wasted work. Fresh eyes matter here more than anywhere, you read what you meant instead of what you wrote, so set the pages aside for a stretch or bring in another reader before this pass.

## Task

Line-edit a chapter to submission standard. Cut filtering and filler, strengthen the verbs, hunt your repeated tics, and read it aloud to catch what your eye misses. Then run the copyedit for correctness and consistency.
