# Week 6 — Desire and Voice

## Desire drives everything

A character who wants nothing generates no story. Desire is the engine, so give your protagonist one dominant desire that organizes the whole book, then let smaller desires shift scene to scene. The dominant desire should be visible by the end of the first act and shouldn't be fully satisfied until the climax, or should be satisfied in a way that reveals it was the wrong thing to want all along.

Motivation has to be strong enough to justify everything you put the character through. Weak motivation is the most common reason a book feels inert. Ask constantly: why doesn't the character just walk away? If they easily could, the desire isn't strong enough. Turn it up until walking away is impossible.

## Interiority is the novel's superpower

Film can show a face. Only the novel can put the reader inside a mind. This is your greatest tool, so use it. Interiority is the character's thoughts, judgments, memories, and physical sensations on the page. But it's not just reporting feelings. It's the character interpreting the world through the lens of their wound and their want.

Here's the key: two characters in the same room notice different things. What your protagonist notices, ignores, resents, and reaches for characterizes them faster than any description. A jealous man and a grieving woman walk into the same party and see two completely different rooms. Show us the room through the wound, and you've shown us the person.

## Voice starts here

Voice is the personality of the prose, the distinctive way this particular mind perceives and speaks. It comes from committing fully to one specific consciousness. In first person, the voice is the character's outright. In third, it's a blend of narrator and character. Either way, voice emerges when you stop describing the character from outside and start rendering the world as they experience it. Word choice, rhythm, what they find funny, what they can't stop thinking about, that's voice.

## Task

Write your protagonist's dominant desire in one line, strong enough that walking away is impossible. Then write one page of their interiority: the world filtered through their wound and their want, so the voice starts to surface on the page.
