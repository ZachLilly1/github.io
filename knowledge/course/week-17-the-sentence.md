# Week 17 — The Sentence

## The fundamental unit

Everything comes down to sentences. You can have a perfect structure and living characters and still lose the reader if the prose is flabby. This week you sharpen at the level of the line, because this is where competent writing becomes good writing.

## Verbs over modifiers

Strong writing favors concrete nouns and active verbs over adjectives and adverbs. "She hurried" is weaker than "She bolted." "He walked angrily" is weaker than "He stormed." When you find a verb propped up by an adverb, you've usually found a weak verb. Replace the pair with one precise verb and the sentence gets faster and harder. Adverbs are a signal, not a crime, but most of them mark a spot where a better verb is hiding.

## Show and tell, both

The rule "show, don't tell" is half right. You need both. Showing, dramatizing through action, dialogue, and sensory detail, creates immersion and lets the reader draw their own conclusions. Use it for the emotional and important moments. Telling, summarizing directly, creates efficiency. Use it to compress time and skip what doesn't matter. Amateurs tell everything. The overcorrected show everything and drown the reader in unimportant detail. The skill is knowing which moments deserve the slow, dramatized treatment and which get handled in a sentence.

## Specificity and the cut

The right concrete detail does more than a paragraph of abstraction. "A bird" is generic. "A grackle picking at a chip bag" is alive. But detail must be selected, not piled on. Choose the one telling detail that carries meaning or reveals character, and cut the rest. And kill your darlings, the beautiful sentence that draws attention to itself at the story's expense has to go. Style should be a clear window most of the time and stained glass only where the moment earns it.

## Task

Revise a page. Find every weak verb propped up by an adverb and replace it with one precise verb. Cut the filler and the filtering. Land on the few telling details that carry weight and cut the rest.
