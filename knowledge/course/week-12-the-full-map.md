# Week 12 — The Full Map

## The whole novel, beat by beat

You have the pillars and you've chosen your framework. Now you fill in the whole map, because this week ends with a beat sheet for the entire book, a scene-by-scene or beat-by-beat plan from first page to last. This is the map you'll draft from. It doesn't have to be perfect and it will change, but knowing where you're going is what lets you steer.

## Fill between the pillars

Start with your load-bearing beats and fill the space between them. Between the inciting incident and the midpoint, what escalating obstacles does the character face? Between the midpoint and the all-is-lost, how do the walls close in? Each beat should raise the pressure from the one before. If two adjacent beats sit at the same level of tension, one of them is dead. Every step should climb.

## Plant backward from the climax

Here's the professional move. Once you know your climax, work backward. The climax requires certain things to be true, a skill the character learned, an object they'll need, a relationship that pays off. Go back through the map and plant those setups early, so the ending feels both surprising and inevitable. The reader should reach the climax and feel that everything led here, even though they didn't see it coming. That feeling is engineered, not lucky. You build it by planting backward.

## The map is a tool, not a cage

A beat sheet is scaffolding. It keeps you from getting lost in the muddy middle and it lets you spot structural problems before you've written a hundred pages. But it stays flexible. When the draft teaches you something the map didn't know, update the map. The plan serves the book, not the other way around.

## Task

Complete the beat sheet for the entire book. Fill every beat between your pillars, making sure each one raises the pressure. Then work backward from your climax and plant the setups it will need. You now hold a map from first page to last.
