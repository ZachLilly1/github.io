# Week 8 — The Collision

## Wants in collision

You've built a protagonist with a want, an antagonist built to oppose it, and a cast who each want something of their own. This week you set them against each other, because a story is what happens when incompatible wants collide. A room full of people who all want the same thing is boring. A room full of people who want different, conflicting things is a scene waiting to catch fire.

## Map the collisions

Take each significant character and ask what they want from the protagonist, and what the protagonist wants from them. Then find where those wants clash. The mentor wants the protagonist to follow the safe path. The protagonist wants to prove themselves. That's a collision, and it will generate scenes on its own. Map every important relationship this way. You're looking for the friction points, the places where two people can't both get what they want.

## The web, not the spokes

Weak casts are built like spokes on a wheel, every character connected only to the protagonist. Strong casts are a web, where the supporting characters want things from each other too, not just from the lead. The love interest and the best friend have their own tension. The antagonist and the mentor share a history. When the cast is a web of competing wants, the story has depth in every direction, and you can generate conflict from any pairing, not just from the protagonist versus everyone.

## Fill the gaps

As you map, you'll find holes. A character with no want. A relationship with no friction. A player who exists but does nothing to anyone. Those are the gaps to fill now, before you build structure. Either give the character a want that collides with someone, or cut them. Every person on the page should be pressing on someone else.

## Task

Map how each character's want collides with your protagonist's, and where the supporting characters' wants collide with each other. Find the friction points. Fill any gap where a character has no want or no collision, or cut the character. When every relationship has friction, the cast is ready to carry a plot.
