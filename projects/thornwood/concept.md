# Concept Sheet — Thornwood

Status: LOCKED. This is the foundation. It does not change from here. Everything built on top of it (characters, structure, scenes) reads from this page.

- **Working Title:** Thornwood
- **One-Line Premise:** When a violent storm seals fixer Edward Thorne inside Thornwood with a guest who knows every desire he has caged, he must resist the house's escalating temptations and get them both out alive, or be consumed by the thing he buried, but the only door out opens onto the one act he swore never to commit again: letting someone all the way in.
- **Genre:** Horror (primary), Romance (secondary). Gothic, psychological, erotically charged. The horror contract leads.
- **The Promise to the Reader:** Dread that tightens with every chapter, a threat that costs something real, the fear made physical inside one house, and a descent that pays off. The intimacy runs hot underneath, but the primary promise is horror.

## The Protagonist

- **Who he is:** Edward Thorne. The man hired to clean up other people's ruin without ever being touched by it. Immaculate control, spotless record, lives sealed inside Thornwood, the estate he restored stone by stone.
- **The Wound:** Once, he let himself want someone all the way, dropped his control for a single night, and something irreversible happened. Someone was destroyed in the one moment he stopped holding himself back. He decided his desire is a monster and his control is the only cage that holds it.
- **The False Belief:** "If I let anyone in, I destroy them. Wanting is annihilation. The seal is the only thing keeping the monster caged."
- **What He Wants (conscious, external):** To stay sealed and untouched. Finish the job, hold the record, get through the night with his control intact.
- **What He Needs (unconscious, internal):** To let himself be truly wanted, and to want back. To accept his hunger as human instead of monstrous, and survive intimacy rather than flee it or kill it.

## The Force Against Him

- **The Central Obstacle:** Thornwood itself. The house feeds on suppressed desire. The harder Edward denies what he wants, the more the house manifests it: rooms that were never there, a guest who knows his exact hunger, doors that open onto the thing he buried. It tempts him to feast on desire while keeping his heart bolted shut, because indulgence without intimacy is what actually damns you. The one exit is the single act he swore never to repeat: letting someone all the way in.
- **The Guest (the dark mirror of Maeve):** The house wears Maeve's face. The real Maeve is haunted, tender, cunning, ambitious. Her true want is to stop earning the right to be loved and to reclaim the voice that was taken from her. Her wound left fractured trust and a belief that she is too much or dangerous when she is not okay. The house strips all of that out. It offers Edward a Maeve with no wound, no needs of her own, a reflection who only ever wants what he wants and asks nothing back. That frictionless hunger is the seduction and the trap. Whether the guest is ever, at any point, the real Maeve is the question the book holds open.

## The Stakes

- **External Stakes:** Fail and the house keeps him, another permanent guest, and the woman stranded with him dies the way his first victim died.
- **Internal Stakes:** Fail and he confirms the belief forever. He is only a monster, his hunger can never be safely loved, and he dies sealed, never once truly known.

## Why This Book

It takes the fear that the thing you most want is the thing that will destroy everyone near you, and it forces the question underneath: is intimacy annihilation, or is it the only thing that saves you from the monster you become without it.
