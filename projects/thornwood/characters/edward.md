# Protagonist Architecture — Edward Thorne

Built from the inside out. Each layer feeds the next. Reads from the locked concept in `../concept.md`.

- **Name:** Edward Thorne
- **Role:** Protagonist
- **Surface:** A fixer, a recovery man. He cleans up other people's ruin without a mark on him and keeps a spotless professional record. He owns Thornwood, the estate he restored stone by stone, and lives sealed inside it. Cold, cunning, loyal. His loyalty runs to the job and the record, the one bond that has never been able to hurt him.

## The Wound

He loved a woman named Cora. For one night he finally stopped holding back and let himself want her all the way. Thornwood woke to that desire and took her. He watched it happen and did nothing, because in that moment he wanted more than he wanted to save her. She became the house's first permanent guest. He has never forgiven himself for the wanting or for the stillness that followed it.

Then he did the thing that tells you everything about him. He did not flee the house. He restored it. Board by board, room by room, he rebuilt the place that ate her and made it perfect. The restoration is shrine and cage at once. Leaving would mean losing her for good. Staying keeps the monster where he can watch it.

*(Name "Cora" is a working placeholder, changeable. Her connection to the guest who now wears Maeve's face is a thread to develop in the supporting-cast stage.)*

## The False Belief

"If I let anyone in, I destroy them. Wanting is annihilation. My control is the only cage that holds the monster, and the cage can never open again."

## The Coping Behavior

Total control, dressed as discipline. He fixes other people's disasters so he never has to feel his own hunger. He keeps Thornwood sealed, ordered, and immaculate. He allows himself no intimacy of any kind. The perfect record is armor and penance in the same breath: proof that he is a man who contains things, and payment on a debt he can never clear. Under pressure he does not heat up. He goes colder and more precise. His tell is stillness, the held hand, the level voice, the man who refuses to flinch because flinching is the first crack.

## The Want (conscious, external)

Stay sealed and untouched. Finish the job, keep the record clean, get through the night with his control intact and the door still bolted.

## The Need (unconscious, internal)

Accept that his hunger is human, not monstrous. Let himself be truly wanted and want back, and survive intimacy instead of fleeing it or letting it kill. The monster was never the desire. It was desire cut off from any willingness to be known.

## The Arc

Working direction: positive. He must learn that his want was never the thing that damned him. The refusal to be vulnerable is what feeds the house, because Thornwood grows fat on desire severed from intimacy. The only exit is the one act his wound forbids: to want someone fully and stay, to be known instead of sealed. He breaks the bargain by choosing genuine vulnerability, and survives.

The tragic version stays live until structure locks it: he takes the house's offer, feasts on a hunger that costs him nothing and asks nothing back, and becomes Thornwood's next permanent resident. If we run positive, the tragic ending is the shadow the reader fears the whole way down.

## How the Story Forces the Collision

The storm seals him inside with the guest. The house escalates its temptations, and every time he indulges desire while keeping his heart bolted, the house feeds and the trap tightens. His want, to stay sealed, is precisely the thing that damns him. His need, to let someone all the way in, is the only door out and the one thing his wound swore off. The book drives the want and the need into each other until one of them breaks him.

## What the Guest Gets Past His Guard

She knows the exact shape of his hunger and offers it with no cost and no vulnerability required. She wears the face of the thing he has wanted and forbidden for years. Against a woman with her own wound and her own needs he could hold the line forever. Against a reflection that only ever wants what he wants, the line is almost impossible to hold. That is the point, and that is the trap.
